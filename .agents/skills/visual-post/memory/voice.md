# Voice — first person, his own experience

*Given 2026-07-31, after a first pass on five posts came back as "clear it is
generated by an LLM and in 3rd person." Migrated from Claude auto-memory into
this skill 2026-08-01.*

Write **as Lokesh, recounting his own experience** — what he wanted, what
annoyed him, what he tried, what he got wrong, what he still isn't sure about.
Not a case study written about the project.

The specific tells that made the first drafts read wrong:

- **A tidy aphorism closing every section.** "It works. It is not Haskell." is
  fine once; landing every section on a mic-drop is the giveaway.
- **Meta-commentary about the post itself** — "that ratio is the actual subject
  of this post", "this post is mostly about…", "the part worth stealing".
- **Announcing the moral**: "the lesson I took", "the inference I draw", "what I
  think it taught me". Let the reader take the lesson; just say what happened.
- **Pull-quoting my own sentences** as blockquotes for emphasis.
- **Relentless "X. Not Y." and balanced tricolons.** Vary sentence length; let
  some sentences ramble.
- **Section headings that state conclusions** rather than name the topic.
- Intensifier habit: *genuinely, actually, precisely, exactly* on every claim.

What to do instead: open on the concrete moment or irritation that started it,
keep uncertainty in ("I'm fairly sure I didn't know that at the time", "I don't
have a good answer for that yet"), admit the embarrassing parts directly, and
let sections end without resolving. First person throughout.

Captions are prose too. A caption that announces its own significance ("the
moment the design paid off") has the same problem as a section heading that
states a conclusion — say what to look at, let the reader draw the line.

**Why:** it's a personal blog — the value is that it is his experience, and
polish that reads as generated undermines the technical content too.

**How to apply:** facts, numbers, screenshots and diagrams stay exactly as
verified ([[post-shape]]); only the voice changes. After drafting, reread for
the seven tells above and cut them.
